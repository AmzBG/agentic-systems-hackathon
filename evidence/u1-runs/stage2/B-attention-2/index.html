Scaled Dot-Product Attention in Three Matrices

Audience: A second-year engineering undergraduate who knows vectors, matrices and basic probability but has not studied Transformers.

Plan: Learners step through scaled dot-product attention on 3-by-2 toy Q, K and V matrices: raw dot products become a score matrix S, a row-wise softmax turns S into the weight matrix A whose rows sum to one, and A V gives the output. A toggle removes the 1/sqrt(d_k) division and a gain slider amplifies scores so learners see the weights flatten into a uniform row or concentrate on one value row.

Starting point

Idea: Attention output is a weighted average of the value rows, where the weights come from a softmax over query-key dot products divided by sqrt(d_k).

Why it matters: This one equation is the whole attention mechanism: it explains how a query picks which keys matter, how that choice becomes a probability distribution over positions, and how the values are then mixed.

Explanation: Row i of the query matrix Q and row j of the key matrix K give the dot product q_i dot k_j; collecting these for all i and j gives the three-by-three score matrix S, where each score is multiplied by the score gain g and divided by sqrt(d_k) when the scaling toggle is on (d_k is the number of components in a query row, here 2, so sqrt(d_k) is about 1.414). For each row of S the row maximum is subtracted before exponentiating and the exponentials are divided by their row sum, producing the attention weight matrix A; because the subtracted maximum cancels between numerator and denominator, the row sums of A are exactly 1 and are reported as row sums of A. The largest entry of A is reported as largest attention weight. Finally each row of the attention output O = A V is the weighted average of the three value rows of V using that row of A.

Compare your prediction

Observe: Every entry of the attention weight matrix A becomes 0.3333, the largest attention weight readout drops to 0.3333, the row sums of A stay at 1, and each row of the attention output becomes 0.667 and 0.667, the plain average of the three value rows.

Explain: Identical key rows give the same dot product with a given query, so all three scores in each row of S are equal; the softmax of equal scores is the uniform distribution 1/3, so A spreads mass evenly, and A V is just the unweighted mean of the value rows.

Grounding

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, Equation (1) FROM PAPER

    Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q K transposed / sqrt(d_k)) V; the dot products of the query with all keys are divided by sqrt(d_k) and a softmax gives the weights on the values.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, footnote 1 FROM PAPER

    If the components of q and k are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1, their dot product has mean 0 and variance d_k, so for large d_k the dot products grow large and push the softmax into regions with extremely small gradients; scaling by 1/sqrt(d_k) counteracts this.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 SIMPLIFICATION

    This page replaces the growth of dot products with d_k by a single gain slider g that multiplies the three-by-two toy scores, and fixes d_k at 2 so the scaling factor is the constant sqrt(2).

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, Figure 2 left TOY EXAMPLE

    The displayed matrices Q, K and V and the readouts 0.3333, 0.667, 0.9647 and 0.982 are illustrative toy numbers chosen for a three-by-three attention problem, not measurements from the paper.

Limitation

The gain slider g is a teaching stand-in, not part of the paper's formula: the paper keeps the divisor fixed at sqrt(d_k) and notes that dot products grow with d_k (variance d_k for unit-variance components), so raising g mimics the effect of more key components. Here d_k is fixed at 2 by the two columns of Q and K. Subtracting the row maximum before exponentiating is only a numerical device and leaves the weights unchanged. No division can fail: the exponentiated row always contains exp(0) = 1, so every row sum is at least 1, and when all scores in a row are equal (for example when the gain is 0) the softmax correctly returns the uniform distribution.
