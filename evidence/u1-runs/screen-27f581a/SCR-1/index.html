Scaled dot-product attention on a 2x2 query and key

Rows of Q are queries and rows of K are keys; entry S[i][j] is the dot product of query i and key j, and rows of V are the values the weights mix. The shared query/key dimension is d_k = 2, the column count of Q and K.

Why it matters: The defining operation is a softmax applied separately to each row of Q K^T / sqrt(d_k): it converts compatibility scores into nonnegative weights that sum to one per query row, so A = P V is a convex combination of the value rows.

About this lesson

Audience: Engineering undergraduate familiar with algebra

Plan: The page computes scaled dot-product attention for a fixed 2x2 Q and K and a 2x1 V, showing raw scores Q K^T, scaled scores, row-wise softmax weights and output P V, driven by a toggle that removes the 1/sqrt(d_k) factor. Two presets contrast uniform scores and a dominant score.

Mechanism and result

No valid result yet.

d_k = 2 equals the column count of Q and K; S = Q K^T, so S[i][j] = sum over m of Q[i][m]*K[j][m]; S_scaled[i][j] = S[i][j]/sqrt(d_k) when the scaling toggle is on and S[i][j] otherwise; r[i] = max over j of S_scaled[i][j] and E[i][j] = exp(S_scaled[i][j] - r[i]); P[i][j] = E[i][j] / sum over j of E[i][j], then A = P V.

Displayed numbers are rounded; calculations use full precision.

Observe: S and S_scaled have all four entries equal, 2 and 1.414; both rows of P are 0.5 and 0.5, and A is 15 in both rows.

Explain: All four dot products are equal, so each row maximum equals both entries, every shifted exponential is exp(0) = 1, and the row normalization splits the mass evenly; each output row is the arithmetic mean of the two values, 15.

Inspect measured values, expectations and tolerances

    Limitation

    The page fixes d_k = 2 and uses single 2x2 Q and K and 2x1 V matrices, so multi-head projections, masking and any training are absent; these numbers are not the paper's experimental results. The row-maximum subtraction is always applied, which keeps exponentials in (0,1] and avoids overflow for raw scores up to 2e6; the scaling toggle only controls whether 1/sqrt(2) multiplies the scores.

    Grounding

    • FROM PAPER Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (2017) — Section 3.2.1, Equation 1

      Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/sqrt(d_k))V, where the softmax is applied separately to each query row and d_k is the query/key vector dimension.

    • FROM PAPER Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (2017) — Section 3.2.1

      Removing the scaling factor leaves plain dot-product attention over the raw scores QK^T.

    • SIMPLIFICATION Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (2017) — Section 3.2.1, Equation 1 (toy instance)

      The page sets d_k = 2, fixes Q and K at 2x2 and V at 2x1, and uses the row-maximum subtraction as an algebraically equivalent stable form of the row softmax.

    • TOY EXAMPLE Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (2017) — Section 3.2.1

      The default matrices Q = K = [[1,0],[0,1]], V = [[10],[20]] and the exploration presets are illustrative numbers chosen for hand-checkable arithmetic, not paper results.
