Scaled Dot-Product Attention in Two Dimensions

Audience: A second-year engineering undergraduate who knows vectors, matrices and basic probability but has not studied Transformers.

Plan: Compute scaled dot-product attention on small editable Q, K and V matrices: form query-key dot-product scores, optionally divide them by sqrt(dk), softmax each row into attention weights, and take the weighted sum of the value rows. Heatmaps and readouts let the learner compare equal, zero and dominant scores and confirm that every weight row sums to one.

Starting point

Idea: Attention output is a weighted average of the value vectors, and each weight is a softmax over query-key dot products.

Why it matters: Scaled dot-product attention is the one operation repeated throughout the Transformer, and the square-root scaling is the piece most often taken for granted.

Explanation: Q and K are two two-dimensional matrices: each row is a query vector or a key vector. Scores S collect the dot product of every query row of Q against every key row of K; when the scaling switch is on, each dot product is multiplied by 1/sqrt(dk), where dk is the key-dimension control. Weights A apply a softmax to each row of S, so entries of A are nonnegative and each row adds to one, which the readout Row sums R reports. Output O multiplies A by V, so each row of O is the average of the rows of V under the matching row of A.

Explain: Every weight is exp(s)/sum of exp(s) over the same s, so a row of identical scores gives identical weights, here exactly two halves; the output row then sits halfway between the two value rows.

Observe: Scores S show 4 and 0 in each row, Weights A show about 0.982 and 0.018, Row sums R stay at 1, and Output O keeps almost all of value row 1.

Explain: The softmax exponentiates the scores, and exp(4) is about 54.6 while exp(0) is 1, so the first key receives about 98 percent of the weight. Switching scaling on would divide both scores by sqrt(dk), shrinking the gap between them and pushing the weights back toward uniform.

Grounding

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, Equation 1 FROM PAPER

    The output is softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(dk)) V, so query-key dot products are divided by the square root of the key dimension before the softmax.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 FROM PAPER

    Scaling counteracts dot products that grow large in magnitude for large dk, which push the softmax into regions of extremely small gradients.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 and Section 3.2.2 TOY EXAMPLE

    The 2x2 Q, K and V matrices, the editable dk control and the scaling switch are illustrative simplifications of the paper's dk = dv = 64 per head and dmodel = 512 model.

Limitation

dk is a free control here, while in the paper it equals the query and key dimension; no masking, multi-head projection or 512-dimensional embeddings are shown, and the demonstration is a two-row toy. Dividing by the row total never fails because at least one exponential term is positive, and scaling uses a strictly positive dk.
