Scaled Dot-Product Attention in One Step

Audience: A second-year engineering undergraduate who knows vectors, matrices, and basic probability but has not studied Transformers.

Plan: Learners build a tiny scaled dot-product attention step by hand: two queries, two keys and two values. They watch raw dot products become a score matrix, a row-wise softmax turn scores into attention weights that sum to one, and a weighted average of value rows produce the output, with the 1/sqrt(dk) scaling and the largest weight readout as the knob and the gauge.

Starting point

Idea: Scaled dot-product attention scores a query against every key with a dot product, turns those scores into weights with a row-wise softmax, and returns a weighted average of the value rows.

Why it matters: It is the single recurrence-free operation the Transformer stacks everywhere, and its row-wise softmax weighting is what lets every position gather information from every other position in one step.

Explanation: Scores first: entry S[i][j] of the score matrix is the dot product of query row i of Q with key row j of K, multiplied by 1/sqrt(dk) when the scaling toggle is on and left unchanged when it is off. Those scores are then exponentiated row by row, shifted by the row maximum for numerical safety, and divided by their row total to give the attention weights A, so each row of A is a probability distribution over the keys and the row sums read 1. The output matrix is the product A V: row i is the weighted average of the value rows, using the weights in row i of A. The largest weight readout reports the biggest entry of A, so it shows how concentrated that averaging is; raising dk shrinks the scaled scores and lowers that peak.

Observe: Every entry of the attention weights A becomes exactly 0.5, the row sums read 1.0 for both queries, the largest weight readout is 0.500, and because V is the identity matrix the output AV is also 0.5 in every entry.

Explain: A score is a dot product of one query with one key, so two identical keys give two identical scores in that row of S. Softmax exponentiates equal scores into equal numbers, so the row splits its weight evenly; the row sum of A is a probability mass and therefore stays at exactly 1.

Observe: The score matrix S reads [3, 0] in row 0 and [0, 1] in row 1 because no division happens, the attention weights become about [0.953, 0.047] in row 0 and [0.047, 0.953] in row 1, and the largest weight readout is about 0.953. Switching scaling back on with dk = 2 divides each score by about 1.414 and the largest weight drops to about 0.89, while the row sums still read 1.

Explain: Softmax exponentiates the scores, so an unscaled gap of 3 makes the aligned key's exponential about 20 times the other one and nearly all of the row weight lands on key 0. Dividing the scores by sqrt(dk) shrinks the gap before exponentiation, which flattens the weights; this is the effect the paper cites when it says large dk pushes softmax into regions with very small gradients.

Grounding

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, first paragraph FROM PAPER

    The input consists of queries and keys of dimension dk and values of dimension dv; the dot products of the query with all keys are divided by sqrt(dk) and a softmax gives the weights on the values.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Equation (1), Section 3.2.1 FROM PAPER

    The matrix of outputs is Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(dk)) V, which is the order used here: scores, then softmax weights, then a weighted sum of the value rows.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, scaling footnote FROM PAPER

    If components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, the dot product has mean 0 and variance dk, so for large dk the dot products get large and push softmax into regions with extremely small gradients; this motivates scaling by 1/sqrt(dk).

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 (toy instantiations on this page) TOY EXAMPLE

    The Q, K and V matrices, the two-query two-key setting and the free dk slider are illustrative values chosen for a hand-checkable demonstration, not the paper's 8-head, dk = 64 configuration.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2 SIMPLIFICATION

    This page shows one head with no masking, no projections and no residual or layer normalization, so it illustrates only the inner attention function.

Limitation

The two 2x2 matrices fix the actual dot products, while the dk slider only sets the divisor sqrt(dk); in a real layer dk is the number of key components and the scores would grow with it. Single-head attention without masking is shown, and softmax is always well defined here because the row total of exponentials is at least 2 for any finite scores, so no weighting rule ever divides by zero. The row sums reading 1 is a property of the softmax normalization, not evidence about any trained model.
