Scaled Dot-Product Attention by Hand

Audience: A second-year engineering undergraduate who knows vectors, matrices, and basic probability but has not studied Transformers.

Plan: Teach scaled dot-product attention for a tiny set of queries, keys, and values. The learner edits Q, K, and V, toggles the 1/sqrt(d_k) scaling, and follows the calculation from dot products to softmax weights to the weighted output.

Starting point

Idea: Scaled dot-product attention computes each query's output as a weighted average of value vectors, where the weights come from a softmax over scaled query-key dot products.

Why it matters: It is the core operation of the Transformer and shows how a model decides which positions to use when forming a representation.

Explanation: The query matrix Q and key matrix K produce the Dot products QK^T. Dividing each entry by sqrt(d_k) when the Scaling toggle is on gives the Scaled scores. Softmax over each row of Scaled scores gives the Attention weights. Summing each weight row gives Weight row sums, which equal 1. Multiplying Attention weights by the value matrix V gives the Attention output.

Explain: Removing the 1/sqrt(d_k) divisor multiplies the scores by sqrt(2), so the largest score is farther ahead of the others and softmax concentrates more weight on it.

Grounding

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1, Equation (1) FROM PAPER

    The paper defines Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(QK^T/sqrt(d_k))V, with queries and keys of dimension d_k and values of dimension d_v.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 FROM PAPER

    The dot products are divided by sqrt(d_k) to counteract large dot products that push the softmax into regions that have extremely small gradients.

  • Attention Is All You Need — Section 3.2.1 SIMPLIFICATION

    The page uses toy 2x2 queries, 3x2 keys, and 3x2 values so that every score, weight, and output entry is hand-checkable.

Limitation

The demonstration uses two queries, three keys, and two value components; real Transformer heads use dimensions such as 64 and many sequence positions. The softmax subtracts the row maximum for stability, a step that leaves the mathematical weights unchanged. All values are unitless and not trained.
